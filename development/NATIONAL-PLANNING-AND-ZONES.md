# National Economic Planning, Productive Development and Zones

## Purpose
Coordinate long-term investment in productive capacity while allowing businesses, communities and local governments to experiment and adapt.

## Rolling planning cycles
Consider rolling five-year national development cycles with:
- Clear national outcomes and measurable targets.
- Publicly explained investment priorities.
- Sector strategies and skills plans.
- Annual progress reviews and independent evaluation.
- Ability to revise targets when evidence or conditions change.

## Development zones
Retain development zones as a central instrument to concentrate infrastructure, skills, services and investment around viable productive activities.

Potential zone types to assess:
- Agro-processing and food value chains.
- Manufacturing and light industry.
- Logistics and trade.
- Renewable energy and related services.
- Technology and business services.
- Resource processing and responsible value addition.

Zones should not be created merely by drawing boundaries or granting tax exemptions. Each needs a credible economic case, infrastructure plan, workforce strategy, environmental safeguards, transparent incentives and measurable local benefits.

## Local and district experimentation
Allow districts and municipalities to test solutions suited to their conditions. Successful pilots can be adapted elsewhere; unsuccessful pilots should be evaluated openly.

## Productive development priorities
- Increase domestic production where competitive and sensible.
- Add value to local agricultural and mineral resources.
- Strengthen suppliers and small businesses.
- Improve logistics, energy, water and digital infrastructure.
- Build technical and managerial skills.
- Expand exports and regional trade.

## International case studies
Study China's planning cycles, development zones, infrastructure investment, export development and local experimentation as one reference case. Do not assume its institutions can or should be copied wholesale. Compare with other countries and assess governance, fiscal, social and environmental outcomes.

## Accountability
Publish project selection criteria, costs, contracts where appropriate, targets, beneficiaries and performance reviews. Incentives should be time-limited and tied to verified outcomes.
