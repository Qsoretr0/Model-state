# AI, Automation and the Distribution of Wealth

## Challenge
AI and automation may increase output while changing the demand for certain kinds of labour. The central question is not only how many jobs change, but who owns the systems and assets that generate the gains.

## Proposed policy directions
1. Prepare people for changing tasks through accessible lifelong learning.
2. Support transitions between occupations and sectors.
3. Broaden access to productive capital and investment.
4. Explore public, community and worker participation in returns from selected productive assets.
5. Maintain essential services and income protection through economic transitions.
6. Encourage local capacity in digital infrastructure, applied AI and technology services where viable.

## Distribution channels to investigate
- Broad-based investment accounts.
- Worker ownership and profit-sharing.
- Cooperative enterprises.
- Community or national development funds.
- Dividends from carefully selected public or collectively owned assets.
- Tax systems that capture economic rents and extraordinary gains fairly.

These are options for evaluation, not settled policy choices.

## Avoid simplistic assumptions
AI will not affect every occupation in the same way or on the same timetable. Some tasks will be automated, others augmented, and new activities may emerge. Policy should be based on sector-level evidence and scenario analysis.

## Open questions
- Which assets should be publicly, privately, cooperatively or jointly owned?
- How can small investors participate without taking unsuitable risks?
- How can competition and innovation be maintained?
- What tax and regulatory changes are feasible?
- How can productivity gains be measured and shared?
