# Universal Essentials and the Basic-Needs Floor

## Proposal
Establish a reliable floor of essential support so that no person is pushed below a dignified minimum because of unemployment, disability, caregiving responsibilities, economic shocks or technological change.

The exact mix may combine direct income support with universal or subsidized basic services.

## Potential components
- Food and nutrition security.
- Safe water and sanitation.
- Basic healthcare.
- Essential education and lifelong learning.
- Access to safe, adequate housing.
- Basic energy and connectivity.
- Essential transport access where needed.

## Income support and services
A cash benefit can give people flexibility, while direct public services can help ensure that essential goods are actually available and affordable. The right balance requires local cost, capacity and market analysis.

## Unconditionality
The basic-needs floor must not be conditional on merit points, work history, social reputation or participation in a platform. Contribution recognition is a separate layer.

## Delivery options to evaluate
A card or digital wallet could make benefits easier to access, but digital delivery must not exclude people without smartphones, reliable connectivity, formal banking or digital literacy. Offline and assisted access are important.

## Risks and questions
- What is the realistic minimum benefit or service package?
- How should eligibility and residency be defined fairly?
- How can benefits be indexed as prices change?
- How can fraud be reduced without humiliating or excluding recipients?
- What supply-side measures are needed so cash support does not simply raise prices where supply is constrained?
- How should the system interact with existing social protection?

## Evaluation
Model poverty reduction, household welfare, labour-market effects, inflation risks, administrative cost, fiscal sustainability and distributional outcomes before scaling.
