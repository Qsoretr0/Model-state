# Waste-to-Value Economy and Circular Production

## Core idea
Treat waste as a potential stream of recoverable materials and useful inputs, not only as something to collect and dump.

## System architecture
1. Reliable household and commercial collection.
2. Separation at source where practical.
3. Sorting and material recovery facilities.
4. Composting and organic-waste processing.
5. Recycling and reprocessing where markets and quality standards support it.
6. Safe treatment and disposal for residual and hazardous waste.
7. Procurement and market development for recovered materials.

## Potential value streams
- Collection and service contracts.
- Sale of sorted recyclable materials.
- Compost and soil amendments from suitable organic waste.
- Reusable and refurbished goods.
- Recovered materials for local manufacturing.
- Energy recovery only where technically, economically and environmentally justified.

## Design principles
- Protect workers and nearby communities.
- Prevent unsafe informal handling and exposure.
- Do not assume every waste stream is profitable.
- Confirm buyers, quality standards, transport costs and processing economics before investing.
- Prioritize waste prevention, reuse and recycling before disposal or energy recovery.

## Adaptation, not copying
International examples, including commercial waste industries, can inform business models and operations. The system must be adapted to local waste composition, settlement patterns, regulation, infrastructure and market size.

## Research questions
Map waste volumes, current collection systems, disposal sites, material buyers, informal-sector roles, capital needs and environmental risks.
