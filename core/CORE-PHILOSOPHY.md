# Core Philosophy and Design Principles

## 1. Economic security and human dignity
Every person should have reliable access to the foundations required for a dignified life. Access to essentials should not depend on a person's productivity score, employment status or popularity.

## 2. From labour dependence toward shared productive ownership
Employment remains important, but people should have more ways to benefit from productive assets: businesses, farms, infrastructure, cooperatives, investment funds and technology.

## 3. Productivity gains should be broadly shared
AI, automation, natural resources and public infrastructure can generate substantial value. The framework asks how these gains can improve living standards and expand opportunity rather than accrue narrowly.

## 4. Contribution should be recognized without coercion
Paid work matters, but so do caregiving, parenting, learning, innovation, volunteering and environmental stewardship. Recognition systems must not make essential needs conditional on contribution.

## 5. Build productive capacity
Long-term security requires the ability to produce more of what society needs, improve productivity, add value to local resources, develop skills and compete in external markets.

## 6. Sustainability and stewardship
Water, soil, biodiversity, minerals and other resources must be managed with attention to future generations and ecological limits.

## 7. Practical experimentation
Use evidence, pilots, transparent targets and independent evaluation. Learn from international cases without copying institutions that may not fit local conditions.

## 8. Democratic accountability and safeguards
Public power, funds and data systems require transparency, oversight, privacy protections, contestability and clear routes for appeal.

## Non-negotiable design tests
- Essentials remain unconditional.
- No permanent social class based on a contribution score.
- No single institution should control every economic or digital function.
- People retain meaningful choice and privacy.
- Proposed funding must be credible and publicly explainable.
